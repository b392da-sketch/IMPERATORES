# Q002 — Prearranged signals and battlefield messengers, mid-Republican Roman and Punic armies

**Status:** answered and closed for M0, 22 November 2026
**Needed for:** M0 orders and messengers (specification 5.5)

**Decision it informs:** (a) the fixed menu of meanings a prearranged signal can carry, and its reach; (b) who carries orders across the field, mounted or on foot, and what can go wrong.

**Already in Notion:** Goldsworthy pp. 116–170 on command, staff, dispatch riders (pp. 152–153) and standards; the commander-latency study (Hannibal 217, Cannae, Baecula, Zama, Gaugamela, Sabis, Gergovia, Alesia) in page 03; the distinction between an order decided, sent, received, understood and executed (Phase XII).

**What is needed:**

1. Which battlefield signals are attested for Roman and Carthaginian armies of roughly 220–100 BCE (trumpets, horns, standards, flags or other), and what each is said to have meant: advance, halt, recall, attack, the reserve goes in, and so on. Distinguish attested meanings from modern inference.
2. Examples of a signal heard by the wrong units or misread (Gergovia, 52 BCE, is a later candidate outside the period, usable as a comparison), and of signals agreed before battle for a contingency.
3. Who is reported carrying orders between commander and wings: staff officers, tribunes, cavalry, others; and any case of a messenger lost, delayed or not believed.

**What ends the inquiry:** a list of signal meanings that is defensible for this period (even if short), and a list of who carried messages, each with source grades. Exact timings are **not** needed: messenger speed and signal range will be engineering assumptions tested for sensitivity.

## Claude's notes on the answer (22 November 2026)

Answer saved unchanged in `Q002-signals-and-battlefield-messengers-answer.md`. Spot-checked Livy 30.34.11: "quod Scipio ubi vidit, receptui propere canere hastatis iussit" (Scipio orders the recall sounded for the hastati), as the answer says.

**How it is used:**
- **Signal menu for M0:** (1) recall/disengage (attested meaning); (2) a generic prearranged action cue whose meaning is fixed in the deployment plan, such as "the ambush goes in" or "the reserve goes in" (attested as a practice, medium unknown); (3) cavalry engage (attested meaning for mounted troops). An "advance" cue is available only as a labelled engineering extension. No richer flag or trumpet vocabulary.
- **Carriers:** the commander's aides, and officers passing orders down by role (tribunes, centurions, cavalry officers). Whether a battlefield messenger rides or runs is an engineering option. Speed, range, loss and disbelief are engineering assumptions tested for sensitivity.
- **Advance delegation** (Scipio briefing Laelius and Masinissa before the camp attacks, Polybius 14.4) becomes **conditional instructions issued at deployment**: an officer can be told before battle what to do if something happens, and act on it without a new message. This fits the approved specification (latitude, prearranged signals) and will be built in step 4.
- The answer's negative finding (no verified 220–100 BCE case of a signal heard by the wrong unit or a messenger killed in transit) is recorded: those failure modes remain in the model as possibilities, not documented frequencies.
