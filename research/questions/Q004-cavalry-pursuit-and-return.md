# Q004 — Cavalry pursuit, return and rallying, Second Punic War

**Status:** answered and closed for M0, 22 November 2026
**Needed for:** M0 pursuit and rally (specification 2 and 5.6)

**Decision it informs:** the options a cavalry officer has after beating opposing horse (pursue, stop, turn on the infantry, return to the commander), and what makes each more or less likely.

**Already in Notion:** Hasdrubal's redirection at Cannae and Masinissa/Laelius returning at Zama (page 03, subordinate autonomy); Goldsworthy pp. 228–247 on cavalry, pursuit and reserves; Phase XXIX on retreat outcomes depending on the pursuer's options; Xenophon *Hipparchicus* and Arrian on controlled pursuit (prescriptive texts).

**What is needed:**

1. Cases, roughly 220–100 BCE, of victorious cavalry that pursued off the field, stopped, or turned against infantry, with what the sources give as the reason (orders, plunder, terrain, the state of the horses, distance).
2. Cases of routed cavalry rallying and returning.
3. Where the sources' explanations are likely hindsight or praise and blame.

**What ends the inquiry:** 6–10 cases in a table with outcome, stated reason, and source grade. Enough to check that the simulation's options and influences are plausible; no rates or probabilities are expected.

## Claude's notes on the answer (22 November 2026)

Answer saved unchanged in `Q004-cavalry-pursuit-and-return-answer.md`. Spot-checked Polybius 5.85: an elder officer points out that the dust cloud is moving from the phalanx towards Antiochus' camp; he tries to return with his horse guards and finds the army fled. Echecrates waits, sees the dust and the elephants, and takes his cavalry round the flank. Both as the answer says.

**How it is used:**
- **Options for a cavalry officer after beating opposing horse:** continue pursuit; stop; redirect against enemy infantry flank or rear; return to the main field; staged withdrawal under pressure; rally fugitives where support or a camp is near.
- **Influences** are those listed in the answer (orders and latitude, view of friendly infantry, enemy horse's condition, distance, terrain and obstacles, horse fatigue, losses and order, plunder, flank opportunity, perceived reinforcements, a warning or recall). No ethnic pursuit bonus.
- **Rout, then rally, then return to the same battle** is flagged in the code and the record as an **experimental option** without a verified case in the period, per the answer's negative finding. Controlled scenario C6 reports how often it happens, and it can be switched off.
