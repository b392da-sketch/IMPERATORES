# Q003 — Manipular intervals and line relief: M0 answer

**Status:** Answered, 22 November 2026  
**Needed for:** M0 formation and contact switches  
**Decision informed:** Which *alternative* layouts and relief procedures the engine must be capable of representing, **without selecting the historically correct one**.

**Already in Notion:** The Research Hub's military-systems and Roman infantry evidence-to-mechanism material already discusses Polybius 2.30 and 11.22 (light troops passing back through lines), 15.14 (Scipio's reformation), Goldsworthy's photographed discussion of intervals/relief, and the Sabin–Taylor–Zhmodikov–Slavik–Devereaux debate over engagement. The missing deliverable is the **switchable menu** below. All interval widths and rank-change timings remain engineering assumptions, not recovered measurements.

## A. Layout alternatives (three)

1. **Staggered manipular grid with modest gaps maintained during contact.** The three lines are offset and passageways remain between first-line maniples, covered by other formed bodies; these are *not* unopposed empty highways. **Ancient support:** Polybius 11.22.9–10 explicitly has skirmishers pass through intervals; Livy 8.8.9–13 describes gaps and retreat into following lines, but retrospectively narrates fourth-century practice. **Modern advocates:** Michael J. Taylor (2014) argues from hypothetical frontages of 320–570 m for modest combat gaps; Philip Sabin (2000) models supporting units deterring penetration while forward bodies maintain intervals. **Objection:** ancient passages prove some intervals at some phases, not that every interval stayed at a fixed width under enemy pressure; a poorly covered gap risks flanking. **Grades:** ancient P11 **read in part**, L8 **read in part**; Taylor **read in part** (published abstract and introduction), Sabin **read in part** (especially pp. 15–16).

2. **Deployed intervals close before or on contact, creating a broadly continuous front.** **Ancient support:** the danger of uncovered flanks and Livy's reference to closing ranks at 8.8.13 fit this as an interpretation, but **no checked ancient passage gives a universal command to close all first-line maniple gaps on enemy contact**. **Modern reconstruction advocates reported in Taylor's introduction:** Connolly/Daly (rear-century counter-march), Quesada-Sanz (lateral expansion), Delbrück (small 'joints' closed as ranks press forward). These are *different hypothesised mechanisms*, not independent ancient reports. **Objection:** movement may be slow/difficult at close range and could obstruct passage by skirmishers or relief lines; varying spacing and terrain complicate a universal closure. **Grades:** L8 **read in part**; Taylor (2014) **read in part**; Connolly, Daly, Quesada-Sanz and Delbrück **known here only from Taylor's checked summary, not read directly**.

3. **Flexible local gaps with no perfectly maintained chessboard.** Maniples can start with staggered intervals, but small gaps open, contract or close unevenly as formations advance, recoil, change facing or drift. **Ancient support:** Polybius' skirmisher passages (11.22), Scipio's deliberate line-extension at Zama (15.14) and Livy's account of ranks losing alignment at Zama (30.34) make formation rearrangement conceivable. They **do not document an exact elastic-spacing drill**. **Modern advocate/analogue:** Sabin (2000) regards local front fluctuations and limited subunit disengagement as plausible; no checked study establishes this entire option as a standard Roman pattern. **Objection:** partly an engineering sensitivity model rather than a positive historically attested formation. **Grades:** P11/P15/L30 **read in part**; Sabin **read in part**.

## B. Relief/engagement alternatives (three)

1. **Front line recoils through the intervals of a rear line, which takes over.** **Ancient support, unusually explicit mechanics:** Livy 8.8.9–13 says hastati retire slowly into gaps among principes; if necessary the principes and hastati subsequently fall back among the triarii, whose ranks close. **Objection:** Livy writes much later about the Latin War (340 BCE), combining an earlier order-of-battle picture with a didactic narrative; this cannot prove identical routine full-line relief in 216 BCE or relief under continuous sword contact. **Modern perspectives:** Sabin (2000) sees relief during lulls as plausible; Taylor (2014) retains possible gaps but his checked abstract does not settle the passage procedure. **Grades:** L8 **read in part**; Sabin **read in part**; Taylor **read in part**.

2. **Following line advances forward or laterally to support/replace engaged segments, not necessarily withdrawing the entire front first.** **Ancient support:** Polybius 15.14.3–5 narrates Scipio recalling hastati, moving wounded, and placing principes and triarii alongside the hastati for the final Zama engagement; Polybius 18.26 describes an officer detaching maniples from one winning sector for another. Neither proves a universal routine *during unbroken contact*. **Modern advocate:** Sabin (2000) proposes second-line units moving into intervals to fight alongside or relieve forward units. **Objection:** movement needs open routes, time, cohesion and sufficient disengagement; a staged Zama reformation is not rapid individual rotation. **Grades:** P15/P18 **read in part**; Sabin **read in part**.

3. **Limited local replacement/recovery during lulls rather than a whole-line exchange.** Small units or subgroups recoil locally, reorganise, then re-engage while nearby troops cover them; replacement of wounded front men may occur when room permits. **Ancient support:** Polybius 11.22 records light skirmishers repeatedly seeking cover and emerging again; **it is not direct evidence of systematic heavy-infantry front-rank rotation**. **Modern advocate:** Sabin (2000) proposes intermittent local charges and disengagements; Goldsworthy's discussion (as recorded in Notion) distinguishes plausible replacement from an unsupported conveyor-belt of continuously revolving sword fighters. **Objection:** precise relief of individual front ranks in sustained close fighting is not directly demonstrated for M0's period. **Grades:** P11 **read in part**; Sabin **read in part**; Goldsworthy **known here from the Notion reading summary**, not reread in the original pages for this answer.
