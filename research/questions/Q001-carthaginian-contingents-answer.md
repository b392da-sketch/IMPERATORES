# Q001 — Carthaginian contingent types: M0 answer

**Status:** Answered, 22 November 2026  
**Needed for:** M0 fictional Second Punic War-style scenario  
**Decision informed:** Select 5–8 distinct contingent/unit types, their approximate equipment and possible formation behaviours, without assigning invented historical strengths.

**Already in Notion:** The Research Hub's military-systems and command workstreams cover Hannibal's heterogeneous army and its non-uniform officer structures; Polybius 3.33 (troop exchanges/garrisons), 3.113–116 (Cannae deployment and Roman-equipped Libyans), and 15.11–16 (Zama's separate echelons). The missing M0 deliverable is the bounded menu below. Numbers in the table are **ancient author-reported**, never measured strengths or suggested simulated unit sizes.

| Unit type | Arms/protection and changes reported | Formation and fighting (including depth) | Reported strength in a named context; what is disputed or unknown | Evidence/source grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Libyan/African heavy infantry** | At **Cannae**, Polybius says Hannibal equipped his Libyans with Roman arms captured in earlier victories; the precise date, completeness and uniformity of re-equipment are not established. Heavy infantry implies shields and close-combat arms, but Polybius gives no inventory for each soldier. | At Cannae deployed in two sections flanking Iberian/Celtic infantry, then struck the Roman sides. Neither normal rank depth nor a generalised *phalanx* drill is specified. | Polybius reports **40,000 Carthaginian infantry in total** at Cannae (3.114), not a measured Libyan contingent count. No defensible specific Libyan block size. | **P3: read in part; direct ancient narrative**, good for deployment, weak for exact equipment mix/depth. |
| **Iberian infantry** | Polybius describes shields broadly similar in size to Celtic shields and Iberian swords capable of thrusting and cutting. Livy's account of troops **sent to Africa**, not stationed with Hannibal at Cannae, specifies **13,850 Spanish shield-bearing infantry** (21.21): author-reported and not a Cannae headcount. | At Cannae mixed/alternating with Celtic infantry in a projecting centre that fell back. Open/close order and rank depth are not quantified. | No securely separable Iberian count within Polybius' **40,000 total foot at Cannae**. Do not convert Livy's African garrison figure into Italian field strength. | **P3: read in part; L21: read in part.** |
| **Celtic/Gallic infantry** | Polybius contrasts Celtic cutting swords with Iberian weapons. His categorical claim that Celtic swords could only cut should not be made an absolute weapon prohibition for M0. Defensive equipment was varied; no standard armour percentage is reported. | At Cannae fought intermingled with Iberians in the forward-bulging centre. The account reports a staged recession under pressure, not a standard ethnic propensity to retreat. Depth and spacing unreported. | Polybius includes Celts within **40,000 infantry total at Cannae**, without an independent count for the Celtic line. | **P3: read in part.** Polybius' weapon contrast is descriptive and may generalise. |
| **Balearic slingers** | Sling and projectiles; armour/protection not described in the cited passages. | Part of the light screen ahead of Hannibal's main line at Cannae. Can harass at missile range and withdraw; fixed ranks/depth unreported. | Polybius reports **500 Balearic troops left in Iberia** (3.33); Livy reports **870 sent to Africa** (21.21). These are **different garrison allocations**, not evidence that Hannibal had 500 or 870 slingers at Cannae. | **P3: read in part; L21: read in part.** No defensible field-contingent size. |
| **Other light infantry / javelin troops (mixed origins)** | Light missiles/spears; precise protection and national composition vary and are not recoverable as one standard type. | Screens before heavy infantry at Trebia/Cannae; skirmishes, disengagement and screening. Do not force a single ethnic label or heavy-infantry formation. | Polybius reports **8,000** light troops (spear-throwers and slingers together) covering Hannibal's deployment at **Trebia** (3.72). This is an **author-reported combined screen**, not 8,000 javelinmen nor a standard unit size. | **P3: read in part.** |
| **Numidian light cavalry** | Mobile mounted troops using thrown missiles in Polybius' Trebia/Ticinus accounts; no securely standardised armour, shield or horse equipment across Numidian allies. | Skirmishes, feigned withdrawals/harassment, flanking and pursuit; at Cannae on Hannibal's right wing. Not necessarily suited to the same prolonged close cavalry fighting as Iberian/Celtic mounted troops. | Polybius reports **1,800 Numidian horse left with Hasdrubal in Iberia** (3.33), not a tally of Numidians at Cannae; **10,000 horse overall** at Cannae (3.114) is for all Carthaginian cavalry. | **P3: read in part.** Distinguish operational behaviour from unproved ethnic bonuses. |
| **Iberian and Celtic cavalry** | Polybius distinguishes these from Numidian horse in deployment and combat role, but the passages checked do not give a sufficiently detailed universal arms-and-armour inventory. For M0, model as cavalry capable of closer mounted engagement without claiming all riders were equally heavily armoured. | At Cannae placed together on the river-side wing; close, hard-fought cavalry combat, then movement across the battlefield and attack on Roman infantry rear. Specific squadron depth/spacing not supplied. | **10,000 total Carthaginian horse** reported at Cannae (3.114), not a count of Iberian/Celtic heavy cavalry. At Trebia Polybius reports **10,000 cavalry overall** (3.72), again not by type. | **P3: read in part.** Mixed mounted contingent; exact armour and subunit structure unresolved. |

**Named but insufficiently characterised for an additional M0 unit type:** Polybius 15.11 distinguishes Carthaginian/native Libyan infantry in Hannibal's second echelon and Italy veterans in his last echelon at **Zama**; his first echelon comprised a **reported 12,000** mixed Ligurians, Celts, Balearic islanders and Moors. The excerpt does not establish separate standardised equipment, formation depth or unit size for each of these groups. They should remain scenario-specific composition labels unless later evidence is required.

**Design alternatives to preserve:** a mixed-identity army with **equipment and role classes** rather than fixed ethnic combat statistics; Roman-equipped versus differently equipped Libyan heavy infantry; distinct missile screens versus close-order foot; lighter skirmishing cavalry versus cavalry capable of sustained close fighting. Maniple-sized blocks on the Carthaginian side are **engineering abstractions**, not an attested universal Punic organisation. Use invented scenario strengths explicitly labelled *scenario settings*, separate from ancient reported totals.

**Inquiry closed for M0:** Seven usable types plus an explicit insufficient-evidence category; no additional numeric or ethnic calibration warranted.
