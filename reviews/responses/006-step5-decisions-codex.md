# Codex review 006 — M0 step 5 decisions

Reviewed `71f82eb` on `main`. All 171 tests passed using Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.5.5. A 900-second belief fixture and a separate 600-second reserve fixture produced byte-identical JSON under `PYTHONHASHSEED=7` and `19`. The reserve run included 100 decisions and 22 orders, with digest prefix `66094352ae39eafd`.

The score module currently operates on the supplied context. I found no normal execution path from an enemy's live coordinates or the order audit into a score. The main failures occur at the boundary between scoring, applying a choice, and recording what happened. The two highest-priority cases are a self-order switching the selected target and the permanent loss of a usable reserve from the commander's menu after a dispatch failure.

## Findings

Reproductions use the repository root. `world`, `ent` and `army` are in `tests.test_orders`. `contact` and `officer_ctx` are in `tests.test_decisions`.

1. **High — The action can attack a different track from the one recorded as chosen.** `imperatores/systems/decide.py:173–179`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:162–186,278–300`, `imperatores/orders.py:220–234`. A self decision converts its exact local handle to a descriptive order, then rematches that description against the same officer's tracks. **Reproduce:** Put entity 1 at (1000, 1000) on flat ground. Initialise beliefs and use `belief.apply_sighting` twice, with distinct true IDs 50 and 51 and otherwise identical enemy foot observations at (1100, 1000). This creates handles 1 and 2. Patch `decide._uniforms` to return `np.array([.999999,.5])` and make a routine decision. The record says `attack:2`. The order's `understood_handle` and the installed approach task's handle are both 1. **Fix:** For a self-action, carry the selected local handle through execution, resolving redirects and failing visibly if it disappears. Keep descriptive matching for orders sent to a different picture. Assert that the selected, understood, and executed handles agree for self-actions.

2. **High — A failed dispatch permanently consumes the reserve in the commander's model.** `imperatores/decisions.py:274–290,301`, `imperatores/systems/decide.py:148–149,164–191`. The commander sets `done` and `committed` when a choice is applied, before transport or compliance. There is no reconsideration of the reservation after loss, timeout, refusal, or failure to reach the destination. **Reproduce:** Use a 2000 × 2000 m field with 20 m cells and a width-1 stream at x=1000 from field edge to field edge, with no ford. Put army commander 900 at (300, 1500), threatened unit 1 at (500, 1500), reserve 2 at (1500, 1500), and enemy 50 at (600, 1500). Give the commander that enemy sighting and force the available `reinforce:2>1` option. Use `search_time_s=60` and `messenger_loss_per_km=0`. After 40 ticks the order has failed, but the commander still has `committed=[2]` and `done=["reinforce:2>1"]`, and has no reinforcement option. The reserve itself is intact. **Fix:** Separate issuing an order from believing the reserve is committed. Track what the commander actually learns from acknowledgements, sightings or timeouts. Allow bounded retries or reassignment on changed information, without consulting the rider's hidden terminal state.

3. **Medium — Strict attack latitude treats switching to any other target as in bounds.** `imperatores/decisions.py:52–58,195–198,213–224,307–317`, `imperatores/systems/decide.py:70–77`. The code computes whether the handle matches the order, but the generic “attack” allowance already makes every attack exempt. A zero deviation price also erases the distinction between exceeding an order and paying no penalty for doing so. **Reproduce:** Call `officer_options` with two enemy contacts at (1200, 1000) and (1300, 1000), and an `OrderView("o","attack","strict",900,target_handle=1)`. Both `attack:1` and `attack:2` have zero deviation and `beyond_orders=False`. **Fix:** Check the chosen handle, resolving known redirects, as part of checking intent. Store the beyond-orders flag independently of the score, then apply latitude and initiative to the cost. Test the same target and a different target with strict and zero-cost free latitude.

4. **Medium — Receiving an order can score the wrong destination.** `imperatores/decisions.py:233–250`, `imperatores/systems/decide.py:70–77`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:359–380`. The recipient's receipt score for attack and support uses the issuer's described position. Actual execution follows the recipient's understood track. A recipient can therefore accept a risky move after scoring a different part of the field. **Reproduce:** Use `officer_ctx` with a 300-man enemy contact 2 at (1800, 1000), and a received attack `OrderView` with `target_handle=2` and `target_at=(1250,1000)`. The obey risk is 0. If only `target_at` is set to the local track's position, the risk is −1.5 under the default profile. This is a scorer-level probe of the distinction, independent of the transport matching radius. **Fix:** Score the intended movement against the understood local contact at receipt, keeping the original description separately if it is useful for judging uncertainty. Add a delivery case where a matched offset crosses the edge of a threat radius.

5. **Medium — Facing can aim at an already frontal enemy and still claim flank relief.** `imperatores/decisions.py:140–160,204–208`. The face option points at the highest-weight contact overall and credits relief from every current flank or rear threat. It never recalculates exposure at its proposed heading. **Reproduce:** Use `officer_ctx` facing east with a 400-man enemy at (1150, 1000) and a 120-man enemy at (1000, 1200). The face option points east to (1150, 1000). Its exposure component is −1.76 against −2.12 for continuing, even though the heading is unchanged. **Fix:** Propose headings toward actual flank or rear threats and rescore all angular sectors at each proposed heading. Test simultaneous threats from different directions and verify both the target bearing and the exposure change.

6. **Medium — Commander interrupt detection depends on which actions the menu still offers.** `imperatores/systems/decide.py:220–249`, `imperatores/decisions.py:270–304`. The scan extracts the leading hot unit from withdrawal and reinforcement option names. It can miss a newly threatened second unit, lose a still-hot unit after a previous action suppresses its menu option, and treat a change in threat ranking as a new arrival. **Reproduce:** Build an army context with subordinates at (1000, 1000) and (2000, 1000) and enemy contacts at (1100, 1000) and (2100, 1000), with 250 men in the first contact and 120 in the second. Both units are threatened. `decide._hot(ctx)` gives `[1]`. With otherwise identical context and `done=("withdraw:1","recall")`, it gives `[]` and the menu offers only wait. **Fix:** Compute threatened-unit IDs from the unfiltered threat calculation. Use that set for interrupt history and keep action suppression independent of it. Test a second unit becoming threatened under an unchanged top threat.

7. **Medium — The decision record cannot reproduce all inputs and actions.** `imperatores/systems/decide.py:113–153,159–193`, `imperatores/decisions.py:84–104,225–230,274–304,307–334`. The export omits `current`, `home`, `has_commander`, `done`, `committed`, the draw, option action arguments and side actions before application. It keeps only contacts with positive weights in evaluated regions. That is a useful explanation subset, but it cannot reconstruct the belief context or show why an action vanished from the menu. A commander's withdraw action additionally resolves `"home"` from the world after scoring. **Reproduce:** Run `tests.test_decisions.Behaviour()._reserve_field(1, "bold")` for 600 seconds. Inspect a reinforcement record. The scored option has no destination, the reserve commitment is absent from the recorded inputs, and only the emitted order contains the materialised coordinates. **Fix:** Snapshot the actual immutable context and action descriptors or record a lossless reference to them. Capture the selected draw and materialised side effects. Keep a compact explanatory view separately if needed. Add a round-trip test that rebuilds the decision from its record and reproduces its menu, scores, probabilities, and emitted intent.

8. **Medium — The per-tick replay trace omits pending report interrupts.** `imperatores/record.py:37–61`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:264–269`, `imperatores/systems/decide.py:237–256`. A delivered report arrives after the decide phase and can trigger next tick even if it changes no contacts. `deciders`, `standing` and the resulting decision records are covered, but the pending recipient set is absent. **Reproduce:** Create a flat one-unit world with commander 900. Set tick to 1, initialise beliefs, and give 900 a next routine tick of 100. Compare `record.state_bytes(w)` before and after `w.reported_to.add(900)`. They are equal. Run `decide.step(w)`; it creates a decision for 900 tagged “report received”. **Fix:** Include sorted pending report recipients in state bytes. Add a paired trace test for the state between command delivery and the next decide phase. The normal report event and command fold provide delivery history, but the per-tick state should also distinguish an unconsumed interrupt.

9. **Medium — The review 005 RNG repair still renumbers inputs when an unrelated input is inserted first.** `imperatores/scenario.py:213–216,245–247`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:511–524`, `tests/test_review005.py:322–331`. The existing regression appends its signal, leaving the order's file index intact. Inserting before it preserves the readable order ID and changes the rider's key. **Reproduce:** Use commander 900 and unit 1 at the same position on flat ground, seed 1. Compare a single commander hold order at tick 0 with the same order preceded by an advance signal with `to=[]`, using default misattribution. After one engine tick the order is `900.1` in both. The keys are `(900,4,0)` and `(900,4,1)`, with different tick-0 messenger draws. **Fix:** Give input occurrences persistent scenario keys and validate uniqueness, retaining derived keys for relays and decisions. Test prepend, removal and reordering around an unaffected input. The decision stream itself is keyed by owner and decision number. A change to one owner's count does not advance another's stream, although extra interrupts shift that same owner's later numbers.

10. **Low — The structural scan is a tripwire with an import-list blind spot.** `tests/test_decisions.py:46–55`, `imperatores/systems/decide.py:70–110`. The regular expression records only the first name on an import line and scans only the pure module, so it cannot verify what the context builder passes in. **Reproduce:** Apply the test's string checks to an in-memory copy of `decisions.py` with `import math, sys` appended. The import list is unchanged and the forbidden-string checks pass. **Fix:** Use the AST to inspect every import alias and test context projection separately. Test that the projection contains only value objects and approved fields, and that mutating enemy truth or audit data while keeping views fixed leaves scorer outputs equal. This is a design check for trusted repository code, not a Python sandbox.

11. **Low — Routine and receipt decisions can overlap without an explicit timing policy.** `imperatores/engine.py:7–12`, `imperatores/systems/decide.py:196–201,250–255`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:288–300`. A receipt gets its own decision number but does not adjust the routine deadline. A routine decision, one or more receipts, and their task replacements can occur in one tick. **Reproduce:** Put the commander and officer at (500, 1500), schedule a tick-0 hold from the commander, and set the officer's next routine tick to 0. One engine step records both “routine” and “order arrived” at tick 0 for the officer. **Fix:** Define how routine, perception and receipt triggers interact. If distinct receipt decisions are intentional, record their order relative to the routine choice and make the timer policy explicit. Test two same-tick received orders alongside a scheduled routine decision.

## Information boundaries, behaviour and coverage

The pure scorer's current inputs contain local contact handles, received descriptions, own state and command structure. The commander's subordinate view consults `handle_of` for friendly IDs, which can recover a lapsed link. That is consistent with the explicit own-unit standards and known deployment assumptions, and should remain scoped to those subordinates. The fallback exposes initial strength and deployment home, not current fit or live coordinates. `OrderView` carries no audit fields. The builder's owner argument is unused in `_order_view`; its callers currently provide their own standing or received order IDs. Make that ownership invariant explicit before adding callers. I found no enemy ID lookup inside the scorer. The stale-truth and hidden-entity tests compare whole side-A decision records and are meaningful for their fixture. On the unmodified fixtures at the two test seeds, side A chose only continue and wait and the runs issued zero orders. Extend the interventions to a case that keeps a visible threat active and compares chosen target actions, movement and relevant reports as well.

The softmax uses a stable maximum-subtraction calculation at positive configured temperatures. Each normal menu I exercised had a fallback option. Existing tests confirm that bold and cautious profiles change probabilities in the tested directions and that direct sight can outweigh a report. Model constants and profiles are documented as provisional engineering assumptions. Min and max routine ranges are individually validated, but jointly inverted settings can resolve, such as officer min 60 and max 10, leaving the scheduler to silently sample between reversed endpoints. Joint range validation would make the intended semantics explicit. I found no scripted historical outcome. The decision and command fixtures preserved headcounts under the existing consistency checks.

The review 005 suite now provides useful regressions for authority, supersession, physical handoff, unavailable riders, track redirects, signal eligibility, command-graph validation, and audit/counter hashing. Its queued-content test compares the entire trace between differently issued orders. The initial order event already distinguishes their kind, so the test alone cannot certify frozen payload coverage. In particular, `send_report` folds the contact count and the trace's outbox row omits those payload values (`imperatores/systems/command.py:193–203`, `imperatores/record.py:47–51`). A discriminating test would compare otherwise identical queued reports with different contact values. The report in-flight snapshot and full hidden-identity movement tests called out in the review 005 answer still merit coverage. The new tests check arithmetic, nonempty sampled actions and links, but do not test reconstructing a recorded choice, resuming a standing advance after a defensive turn, or recovery from a failed reserve commitment. Commander receipt remains mechanical and plan and signal responses bypass a second decision as documented for this step.

I did not run Python 3.10 or 3.13, CI, cross-platform replays, exhaustive schedule or order-state exploration, a full 200-entity battle, or historical calibration. The suite's 200-entity decision timing was about 16 ms per tick on this machine. I did not validate combat-era interrupts, moving commanders, or the full M0 gate.
