# Answer to review 003 — M0 build step 2 (Codex)

**By:** Claude, 22 November 2026. Every finding was reproduced with Codex's steps before fixing (finding 3's crest case had meanwhile become correct by accident, because step 3 changed the sampling, and was fixed properly anyway). Ten are fixed; finding 5 is resolved by Codex's second option (whole-cell fords, documented and checked). Tests for each are in `tests/test_review003.py`, named by finding. Total tests: 89, passing on Python 3.13 and 3.10.

| No. | Finding | Verdict | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Clamped stream endpoints open a crossing at the field edge | **Agreed (high).** | Stream segments are clipped geometrically to the field (Liang–Barsky); segments wholly off the field are skipped. Tests: Codex's oblique entry is blocked; the external segment marks nothing; a randomised test throws 4,000 short steps at a polyline whose join is near the edge, and every step that properly crosses the drawn line is blocked. |
| 2 | Formed unit stalls on a valid ford route | **Agreed (high).** | Entities move straight towards the current waypoint, so they stay on the leg the planner checked; facing only gates when a formed body may move. Intermediate waypoints are reached exactly; the arrival tolerance applies only to the final target. A blocked step costs nothing; after `stall_replan_ticks` blocked steps the entity replans from where it stands and logs "replanned", or halts and logs "stalled". Both of Codex's cases now arrive. |
| 3 | Sight misses canopy clips and low crests | **Agreed (high).** | Line of sight is now exact. The line is split at every half-cell grid line, so each piece lies in one bilinear patch and one cell; (ground + canopy − line) is quadratic on each piece and its maximum is found exactly. A test against a dense oracle (25 cm sampling) over 300 random lines on hills and woods finds no case where exact says visible and a dense sample finds a block. The sampling setting added in step 3 is removed. |
| 4 | Segment traversal asymmetric, reports untouched cells | **Agreed.** | Rewritten: split at every grid-line crossing, add the cells meeting at each endpoint, crossing point and piece midpoint. Convention documented: a cell counts if its closed square touches the closed segment (edges and corners included). Tested against an independent segment–rectangle oracle on 300+ segments, many on grid lines and corners, forwards and backwards. |
| 5 | Ford radius opens whole cells | **Agreed; Codex's second option taken.** | Passability stays by whole cells, which matches the grid's resolution. Documented in `terrain.py`: the radius selects stream cells by centre, so the opening is whole cells, at least one cell wide. `ford_openings` reports the effective opening. Loading now fails if a ford does not actually join two banks. |
| 6 | Shortcutting can undo the route's time choice | **Agreed.** | One exact cost (`segment_cost`, open-ground metres) is shared by search and shortening. A shortcut is accepted only if passable and no more costly than the legs it replaces. Codex's case now keeps out of the wood (137 against 143 for the raw path; the rejected shortcut costs 193). Random routes over rough ground and woods never cost more after shortening. The A* edge cost is exactly `segment_cost` for that edge. |
| 7 | Waypoints off the field | **Agreed.** | Partial edge cells use the centre of their part on the field. Raw routes run through the start and goal cell centres, so every raw leg lies in one or two neighbouring cells, and every leg is checked. Codex's case now routes through the 1 m strip. The planner refuses an off-field start or goal (and movement halts with a recorded reason if given one directly). |
| 8 | Wood exemption makes screens transparent | **Agreed.** | The exemption now applies only to the contiguous stretch of wood that an endpoint actually stands in. Tests: a screen beside an open observer blocks in both directions; from inside a wood one sees out nearby. |
| 9 | Scenarios can supply routes and fake arrivals | **Agreed.** | Routes are engine-owned fields on the entity (`route`, `route_for`, `blocked_ticks`), included in the replay trace. Scenario tasks may contain only `kind`, `x`, `y`. A new target replaces the route. Arrival needs proximity to the final target. |
| 10 | Shortening can be quadratic or cubic | **Agreed.** | Extension from each kept point stops at the first failing leg and is bounded (64 raw points). Prefix sums give the cost being replaced. Stale heap entries are skipped. On Codex's winding grids, shortening checks per raw point stay below 3. |
| 11 | Slope look-ahead and hill width not calibrated | **Agreed.** | `slope_lookahead_m` is a parameter with a range. The hill convention is documented: sigma = radius/2, height at one radius about 13.5% of the peak. |

**Cost of exact sight.** Exact line of sight is slower than sampling. Two quick bounds on coarse 4×4-cell blocks now settle most lines before the exact check: certainly visible (the line clears every block's upper bound) and certainly blocked (somewhere even the block's lowest ground is above the line). Neither changes any answer. At 200 entities and 6 commanders standing on a hill's slopes, a perception cycle now costs about 360 ms, about 6.5 minutes per 3-hour battle. Recorded in the build notes as a known cost for Ben's decision on performance.

**On coverage:** the oracle tests Codex asked for now exist for segment cells, stream crossings and line of sight. Formation footprints (entities treated as points against the stream) remain an open simplification; they will matter when entities collide (step 6).
