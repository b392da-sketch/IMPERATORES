# Codex review 003 — M0 step 2 terrain, sight and routes

Reviewed `041ee76` on `main`. All 51 tests passed with Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.5.5. The suite's cross-field horse route took 102 ms. Two separate seed-4, three-hour CLI runs with `PYTHONHASHSEED=23` and `89` produced byte-identical JSON, with a replay digest of `f44e3f5e107bc9df…`. Active waypoint lists are included in the per-tick trace through `e.task` (`imperatores/record.py:37–45`).

The stream crossing in finding 1 and the ford stall in finding 2 both occur through the engine. Fix those and the sight false positives before relying on terrain for later orders and beliefs. Reproductions below use the repository root and `P = params.resolve()` unless an override is given.

## Findings

1. **High — Clamping stream endpoints opens a crossing away from any ford at the field edge.** `imperatores/terrain.py:177–190`. Centre-distance rasterisation uses the original polyline. The continuity pass clamps each endpoint coordinate independently, which can rotate a segment away from its actual intersection with the field. **Reproduce:** Build a 100 × 100 m field with 20 m cells and `stream={"width":1,"points":[[50,-100],[90,100]]}`, with no fords. At y = 1 the stream is at x = 70.2, but the continuity pass starts its replacement segment at (50, 0). `t.segment_passable(65,1,76,1)` is true, and `pathing.plan(t,P,"horse",65,1,76,1)` gives the direct route. Start an east-facing heavy cavalry entity at (65, 1), target (76, 1), and tick once with `pace_jitter=0`, `pace_cavalry=5`, and `tick_seconds=5`. It reaches (76, 1). A wholly external segment from (0, −20) to (100, −20) in the same field also creates a phantom stream row. **Fix:** Clip segments geometrically to the field for centreline traversal, skipping those with no intersection. Test an oblique entry, an external segment and a polyline with a join near the edge.

2. **High — A formed unit can stop permanently while following a valid ford route.** `imperatores/systems/movement.py:65–84,98–102`. A unit can advance while its heading differs from the planned segment. The displacement can leave it with no straight passable segment to its next waypoint. A blocked step keeps the same task and never reports the stall. **Reproduce:** Use a 100 × 100 m field, 20 m cells, a width-1 stream from (50, 0) to (50, 100), and `fords=[[50,50,1]]`. Set `pace_jitter=0`. Start east-facing heavy infantry at (21, 22) with goal (75, 2). The planner gives [(50, 50), (70, 50), (75, 2)], and all three legs pass the terrain check. After 180 ticks the entity is still at approximately (38.9382, 37.2425), aiming for (50, 50), with no event. The straight line from its actual position now clips a stream cell before entering the ford. With the same map, start (21, 2) and set `arrival_tolerance=20` to reproduce a second stall after the follower drops (30, 50) before reaching the safe turn. **Fix:** Keep motion on the validated leg, or wheel without translating when a step would leave its passable approach. Apply destination tolerance separately from intermediate waypoints and only skip a turn if the actual next leg is passable. Detect, replan or log persistent stalls.

3. **High — LOS exposes targets behind narrow canopy intersections and low ridge crests.** `imperatores/sight.py:32–49`, `imperatores/terrain.py:57–68`. Uniform samples about half a cell apart miss short clips through a wood cell and can undersample the maximum of the interpolated ground. Either false positive can put a hidden target into a step 3 belief picture. **Reproduce:** On a flat 240 × 80 m field with 20 m cells and `woods=[[100,40,120,60]]`, call `sight.line_of_sight(t,P,11,48.95,2,211,28.95,2)`. It returns true even though the two-metre ray enters the 12 m canopy near (100.25, 40.025), more than 60 m from both endpoints. Separately, build a flat 60 × 20 m field with 20 m cells and set `t.elev[0,1]=2.1`. LOS from (10, 10) to (55, 10), with heights 2 at both ends, returns true even though the interpolated crest at (30, 10) is 2.1 m. **Fix:** Traverse wood-cell intervals and evaluate ground against the ray across each bilinear interpolation patch. Ground minus ray is at most quadratic inside a patch. Test its interval endpoints and any interior maximum. Add short grazing and near-threshold crest regressions.

4. **Medium — Segment traversal can reject the same segment in only one direction and report untouched cells.** `imperatores/terrain.py:80–128`. A mixed-sign corner at the end can step beyond the endpoint and miss the loop's goal index altogether. Axis-aligned segments on grid lines and zero-length corner queries use only the floor-assigned side, despite the broader “touches” wording. **Reproduce:** With 20 m cells, `cells_on_segment(t,10,30,20,20)` yields `[(1,0),(1,1),(0,0),(0,1),(0,2)]`, including (0, 2), which lies beyond the endpoint. Reversing yields only `[(1,1),(1,0)]`. Mark (0, 1) as STREAM and `segment_passable` gives false forward and true in reverse. `cells_on_segment(t,20,1,20,19)` yields only (0, 1). `cells_on_segment(t,20,20,20,20)` yields only (1, 1). **Fix:** Iterate boundary crossings within the segment parameter interval. Specify how endpoint, edge and corner contacts count, including the outer boundary, and apply that convention in both directions. Check the resulting cell sets against an independent segment/rectangle intersection oracle.

5. **Medium — Ford radius opens whole cells and can permit crossing outside the drawn ford.** `imperatores/terrain.py:191–195,73–77`. The radius selects stream-cell centres. A selected cell is traversable across its full area, so the effective opening can extend beyond the radius or disappear entirely in another grid alignment. **Reproduce:** Use the map from finding 2. `t.segment_passable(39,41,61,41)` is true although it crosses the stream centreline at (50, 41), 9 m from the centre of a 1 m ford. This is a crossing through a raster FORD cell. In a separate check of a fully in-bounds joined stream with vertices (20, 0), (55, 45), (80, 100) and no ford, 5,022 randomly generated segments properly intersected the geometric centreline and all were blocked. **Fix:** If ford geometry defines passability, clip the crossing to that geometry or refine the grid. If whole cells define passability, document that scenario authors specify a centre-selection radius, expose the effective opening, and test that each ford actually connects both banks.

6. **Medium — Shortcutting can undo the route's travel-time choice.** `imperatores/pathing.py:44–48,95–108`. Matching the slowest tile on a stretch permits replacing a brief slow portion with a long one. The A* Euclidean heuristic is consistent and admissible for the implemented centre-edge cost because all positive pace factors are at most 1. That edge cost charges the whole edge at the slower endpoint speed, so it is already an approximation to time through each cell. **Reproduce:** Build a 120 × 40 m field with 20 m cells and `woods=[[0,0,60,20],[0,20,20,40]]`. Route formed foot from (10, 10) to (110, 10). With `unittest.mock.patch.object(pathing,"_segment_ok",return_value=False)` to inspect the pre-shortening path, the waypoints are (30, 30), (50, 30), (70, 30), (90, 10), (110, 10). The normal result is a single straight leg. Sum each leg's in-cell lengths divided by the pace factor. The raw path costs about 142.83 equivalent open-ground metres and the shortcut costs 192.86, about 35% more. **Fix:** Share a segment cost between the search and shortener, and accept a shortcut only if its cost is at most the cost it replaces. Keep passability independent of cost. Include rough/wood combinations and varied endpoints in route-cost tests.

7. **Medium — A* can emit waypoints beyond the field from legal inputs.** `imperatores/terrain.py:48–49,153–159`, `imperatores/pathing.py:51–55,95–108`. Partial boundary cells use nominal centres beyond the field. When segment validation rejects every candidate the shortener still emits the next waypoint. **Reproduce:** Build a field with width 41, height 100, cell 20, and a width-1 stream from (0, 50) to (39, 50). Route from (30, 30) to (30, 70). The output is [(50, 30), (50, 50), (50, 70), (30, 70)]. All four legs fail `segment_passable`, though a valid path exists in the 1 m strip near x = 40.5. **Fix:** Use centres inside clipped cells, and verify returned legs against their actual endpoints even when fallback is required. Validate start and goal in the planner's public entry point instead of relying on index clamping and the scenario loader.

8. **Medium — The wood exemption can make intervening woods transparent from open ground.** `imperatores/sight.py:24–29,41–46`. Every sample within the in-wood range of either endpoint ignores canopy, including when both endpoints are in the open. **Reproduce:** On a flat 400 × 400 m field with 20 m cells and `woods=[[100,0,120,400]]`, LOS at height 2 from (90, 10) to (350, 10) is true. The entire screen lies 10–30 m from the observer and its canopy vanishes under the exemption. Reversing at the same heights also gives true. **Fix:** Restrict the exemption to an endpoint actually in a wood and to its contiguous end section. Decide whether range should depend on distance inside woods or total separation. Test an open observer beside a screen, a target just inside its edge, and swapped endpoints and heights. Reversing eye to target with their respective heights exchanged gives the same physical ray. In gameplay different eye and target heights can legitimately give directional visibility.

9. **Medium — Scenario tasks can supply the planner's internal route and fabricate an arrival.** `imperatores/scenario.py:44,67–68`, `imperatores/systems/movement.py:62–72`. **Reproduce:** Add `"route":[]` to entity 1's task in a temporary copy of `scenarios/test_field.json`. Load and step once. It logs arrival at (1000, 1500) for target (3300, 1500) without moving. Supplied legs bypass planning, and a stale route can survive a target edit in place. **Fix:** Keep planned paths in engine-owned state associated with the originating destination or order ID. Validate or ignore runtime-only keys at load. Require target proximity before emitting arrival and invalidate the route on a new order.

10. **Medium — The shortening pass can dominate planning time on winding routes.** `imperatores/pathing.py:65–88,96–108`. It recomputes the minimum across each candidate stretch before checking geometry. With O(n) retained bends, the backward scan can make O(n²) attempts over n raw points and spend O(n³) work rescanning stretches in the worst case. The heap also re-expands stale entries. **Reproduce:** For square grids of side 9, 15, and 21 cells, fill every odd row with STREAM except one gap at alternating ends, first gap at the right. Use 20 m cells, start (10, 10) and goal (20n − 10, 20n − 10). Count calls using `patch.object(pathing,"_segment_ok",wraps=pathing._segment_ok)`. I observed 202, 701 and 2,422 calls and about 5, 27 and 167 ms respectively. **Fix:** Bound shortcut attempts and use cached or incremental stretch costs. Discard outdated heap entries. Benchmark search and shortening separately against winding grids and batches of orders.

11. **Low — Slope sampling and the hill width convention lack explicit calibration.** `imperatores/systems/movement.py:91–96`, `imperatores/terrain.py:8,162–165`, `imperatores/params.py:48–78`. All new registry entries have ranges, units where applicable, and an engineering-assumption basis. The 5 m gradient look-ahead and Gaussian `sigma = radius/2` still affect results without a documented tuning basis. **Reproduce:** Inspect the literal in `ahead = min(5.0, dist)` and evaluate the Gaussian at a sample centre one radius from a hill centre. Its contribution there is about 13.5% of the specified peak. **Fix:** Put the slope look-ahead in the registry with a range and basis. Define what scenario hill radius means and document its width convention. If sampled LOS remains as a performance option, document the error and the resolution setting.

## Test coverage and scope

The supplied tests establish the basic terrain fixture, one thin diagonal barrier, hill and wood occlusion at generous margins, and arrival by two units across one ford map. The stream test uses an analytic side test and counts crossings. The route test samples at roughly 5 m intervals, so a clipped corner can escape it. The one symmetry test compares an obvious block on both sides. The four terrain travel tests use relative pace, order or arrival outcomes and do check those fixture effects. The suite has no independent geometric oracle, graph-optimality comparison, exact completed-step ford check, or adversarial performance fixture. The full JSON cross-process replay check strengthens the same-process digest check for the tested scenario.

I checked load and movement invariants through the existing suite and the targeted cases above. I found no scripted tactical outcome in these systems. Movement uses the stable-ID random stream and the route search itself consumes no randomness. Known terrain is an explicit assumption. Belief isolation, recognition errors and true-state access in decision code remain for later steps. I did not run Python 3.10 or 3.13, compare platforms, exhaustively verify geometry, profile a 200-unit batch, test formation footprint clearance or validate historical calibration. The current barrier checks entity centres. This review does not establish gate G0.
