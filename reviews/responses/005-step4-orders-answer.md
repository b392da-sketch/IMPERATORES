# Answer to review 005 — M0 build step 4 (Codex)

**By:** Claude, 22 November 2026. Findings 1–5, 7, 10 and 11 were reproduced with Codex's steps before fixing. Findings 6, 8 and 9 were confirmed by reading the code paths Codex named, and are covered by new tests. All eleven are fixed. Tests marked "Codex 005/N" are in `tests/test_review005.py`. Total tests: 154, passing on Python 3.13 and 3.10. Codex's fixture style (an order, a report and a recall over 300 s) gives the same digest under `PYTHONHASHSEED=7` and `19`.

| No. | Finding | Verdict | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | An enemy can be ordered; live friendly positions leak | **Agreed (high).** | Authority is checked at load and in `command.issue`/`send_report`. A commander orders only someone in his command, at any depth; an entity's officer orders only himself; reports go to a commander on the same side; signals are addressed within the issuer's command. Every entity now has a plan with a home (its deployment position by default). A rider goes to the sender's own track of the unit if it is more recent, else to that home. Live positions are never read. I tried a third source, the place the sender last ordered the unit to, and dropped it: it sent riders carrying an order to where that order would take the unit, not where the unit was. The fixed, known positions of friendly commanders stay a documented M0 assumption. |
| 2 | A new delivery displaces a waiting order silently | **Agreed (high).** | Policy: a later order supersedes one still waiting to execute. The displaced order fails, once, with "superseded by a later order" and `superseded_by`, and an `order_superseded` event. Tests: two inputs, two messengers in one tick, a recall signal replacing a waiting order, and destruction while waiting. |
| 3 | Unreachable riders and empty routes strand messages and staff | **Agreed (high).** | A used-up route is replanned whenever the goal is still away. The 20 m retarget literal is now `messenger_retarget_m`. A rider with no progress for `search_time_s` gives up ("could not reach the recipient"). Riding back, the same rule makes him *stranded*. A queue fails when the sender is dead, when none of his messengers is left, or after `messenger_wait_timeout_s` (new, 1800 s, assumption). Staff conservation (at hand + out or gone = initial) is part of every consistency check. Tests: Codex's stream fixture drains, with both riders home; the 15 m shift case delivers; all riders caught means the queue fails at once; a rider with no way back is stranded and still counted. |
| 4 | Delivery skips access, liveness and interception | **Agreed (high).** | Each tick, a rider looks, moves, runs his risks, and only then may hand over. A handoff needs the recipient within `deliver_radius_m`, open ground between them, and the recipient alive. Otherwise the message fails ("the recipient is dead" or "has no men left fit"). Dead issuers decide nothing, and their queues are dropped. A rider returning to a dead sender is *dismissed*. In-flight orders survive the sender's death: the rider carries on. Tests: no handoff across the stream; over 12 seeds, a rider caught in the handoff tick never delivers, and some are caught; a dead wing commander receives and relays nothing; a dead issuer sends nothing. |
| 5 | Approach crashes after a small belief update | **Agreed (high).** | A used-up route is replanned. Reaching the end of a route planned for an older goal clears it rather than popping the last waypoint. Test: Codex's case now closes on the updated point. |
| 6 | Lifecycle accepts repeats; impossible moves recorded as executed | **Agreed.** | Status changes go through `orders.transition`, which allows only the legal transitions and raises otherwise; each order ends exactly once. Delivery is idempotent by message id. A place order is executed only once a route exists, and the route is handed to movement. Places in inputs and plans must be passable at load. Tests: a repeated delivery does nothing; an illegal transition raises; a place across an unforded stream fails "no route to the place"; an off-field place is refused at load. Later events on the march (stall, lost target) remain events linked to the order, not changes to its outcome. |
| 7 | Hidden identity changes local handles | **Agreed.** | Links to bodies not seen within `track_gap_s` are dropped for every owner before a batch of sightings is associated. Association then sees the same tracks whatever the hidden id. A recognised body takes its former track by identity (that is what recognition models). Merging leaves a redirect from the old handle, and approach tasks and target orders follow it (`target_track_merged`). Tests: Codex's paired id swap gives equal handles and equal views; an approach task follows a merge. |
| 8 | Replay omits command state | **Agreed.** | Every order transition, audit entry, report, signal, plan firing, launch and messenger status change is folded into a command hash. Tracking-link changes are folded into the belief hash. The per-tick trace adds the sequence counters, messenger keys, routes and route goals, queued message content, full plans, delivered ids and the remaining inputs. The final digest adds the command hash, the order history and the audit. Tests: editing an order's stage detail changes the digest; bumping a counter changes the state bytes; different queued content changes them too. |
| 9 | Signals ignore mounted eligibility; no stable recall home | **Agreed.** | `cavalry_engage` binds only mounted troops; foot record `signal_not_applicable`. A signal binds only if it comes from one of the unit's own commanders, or from an emitter named in a plan entry's new `from`. Otherwise `signal_ignored`. Recall without a plan entry goes to the home (deployment position). Addressed units with no men record `signal_not_received`; a commander named in `to` expands to his units; a dead commander's signal is recorded as not given. |
| 10 | An unrelated signal shifts a rider's draws | **Agreed.** | Random keys no longer come from a shared counter: inputs by their position in the file, relays by parent key plus recipient, plans by entry number, signal responses by signal key plus entity; decisions (step 5) will get their own domain. Readable ids are numbered separately for orders, reports (`.r`) and signals (`.s`), so order ids such as `900.1` are unchanged by signals. Staff contention is unchanged. Test: the paired runs give the same rider key and identical stages. |
| 11 | Command cycles load and crash | **Agreed.** | At load: no id under two commanders or twice under one; an army commander commands wings and entities, a wing commander only entities; cycles are refused, with a separate check besides the rank rule. Command traversal has a visited guard. |

**On the information-boundary notes.** Which body the issuer meant, and which the recipient took, moved off the `Order` into `w.audit`, which no decision code may read; step 5's static scan will enforce that. `check_plans` still reads only the entity's own state, the clock and its own tracks. The messenger system's physical checks (sight of the recipient, access, interception) stay physical and never enter an officer's picture.

**Not done, and why.** Returns under a moving sender wait for moving commanders. Codex's other test-coverage gaps — the sender's picture changing while a report is in flight, and full approach trajectories under an id swap — are noted for step 5's paired-intervention tests.
