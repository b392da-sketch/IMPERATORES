# Codex review 005 — M0 step 4 orders, messengers, signals and plans

Reviewed `1961cbf` on `main`, including the review 004 fixes and step 4. All 126 tests passed on Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.5.5. A 300-second fixture with a delivered order, a delivered report and a recall produced byte-identical JSON under `PYTHONHASHSEED=7` and `19` (digest prefix `3b21f13bb365132d`).

The attack and support paths normally select a description from the issuer's view, match it against the recipient's view and move using the resulting local track. Changing an unseen enemy's coordinates does not change the destination of an already selected track by itself. The main exceptions are addressing and track association. I also reproduced orphaned orders, messages delivered across a barrier or to a dead commander, and a crash when an approach exhausts its old route.

The examples below use the fixture helpers `world`, `ent`, and `army` from `tests.test_orders`. An order input has the form `{"at_s":0,"order":{"issuer":900,"recipient":1,"kind":"hold"}}` unless stated otherwise. Coordinates are in metres.

## Findings

1. **High — A commander can read an enemy recipient's true location and issue a binding order to it.** `imperatores/scenario.py:133–157`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:59–73,108–137,172–196`. Input validation checks that the IDs exist but never checks their side or authority. At launch the fallback called “own deployment” reads the recipient's current position. Even for a friendly addressee, an unseen unit without a plan can silently reveal its live coordinates. **Reproduce:** Use `world([ent(1,"B",310,1500)],[army(commands=())],inputs)`, with an advance-to order from 900 to 1 for (900, 1500). After two ticks the order is executed and the enemy is moving east under it. For the location leak alone, initialise beliefs with `command.belief_mod_ensure(w)` and query `command.believed_position(w,900,1)` before any sightings. Move the addressee without updating observations and query again. The supposed deployment destination follows the move exactly. **Fix:** Validate addressing and authority at load and through the runtime issue/report APIs. Store authorised deployment locations independently of live positions, and use a last-known friendly track or an explicit unknown destination. Keep the fixed friendly commander position exception scoped to the documented assumption.

2. **High — A new delivery silently displaces an order waiting to execute.** `imperatores/systems/command.py:186–196,217–242`. One pending slot per recipient is overwritten without terminating the old order or linking a replacement. **Reproduce:** Give entity 1 two self-addressed inputs at tick 0, advance to (900, 1500) then hold. Start it with `order=0.1` and `order_recovery_per_min_halted=0`. After 400 ticks, order `1.1` still ends at “waiting to execute” with status `understood`, order `1.2` is executed, and `w.pending` is empty. The first can no longer time out or reach the destruction handler. **Fix:** Choose a supersession or queue policy. When replacing an order, atomically terminate the displaced one with a reason and a causal link. Test two messengers in one tick, a signal replacing a pending order, and destruction with a pending order.

3. **High — Unreachable riders and empty cached routes can strand both messages and staff.** `imperatores/systems/messengers.py:53–86,149–167,175–188`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:157–167`. A rider with an unreachable but visible destination does not enter search or start a deadline. An empty cached route also fails to replan for a destination change of at most 20 m. The return path lacks a no-progress deadline. There is no expiry for a queue with no available staff, including when every rider is permanently lost or captured. **Reproduce:** Build a 1000 × 1000 m field with 20 m cells and a width-1 stream from (450, 0) to (450, 1000), no fords. Put commander 900 at (390, 500) and entity 1 at (510, 500). Queue three holds, set `messengers_army=2` and `search_time_s=60`, and run 350 ticks. Both riders remain at x=390 in `riding`, their orders are `sent`, and the third order is still waiting with staff zero. Separately, use a flat map with commander 900 at the default position and entity 1 at (500, 1500). Set `deliver_radius_m=10` and `messenger_loss_per_km=0`. Step once to create the route, move the recipient to (515, 1500), and step another 100 ticks. The rider sits at x=500 with an empty route and the order remains `sent`. **Fix:** Replan an exhausted route whenever completion is still impossible, and apply bounded progress deadlines to outward travel and return. Record a terminal staff disposition when a return fails. Bound dispatch waiting and check available + active + permanently lost against initial staff.

4. **High — Delivery skips physical access, liveness and interception checks.** `imperatores/systems/messengers.py:141–148,168–181`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:172–214,258–270`. Radius alone suffices, before the rider observes, tests access or rolls risk. A dead commander can receive, relay, dispatch and signal. **Reproduce:** In the stream fixture above, place the sender at (439, 500) and recipient at (461, 500). The order is executed at tick 0 across the unforded stream before the messenger moves. On flat ground, put commander 900 and his friendly recipient at x=300 and x=310, both at y=1500. Add enemy light cavalry at (300, 1500), seed 3, `intercept_per_min=3` and `intercept_mounted_factor=4`. The order executes at tick 0 although `messengers._risk(w,w.messengers[0],0)` gives `captured` for that same tick. Finally, put commanders 900 and 910 and recipient 1 at (500, 1500), let 910 command 1, set `w.commander(910).alive=False` and send a hold to 910. Both the parent and 910's child execute within three ticks. A rider that has already entered `captured` is skipped by the step loop. **Fix:** Require a valid physical handoff and a live authorised receiver, and define interception priority at handoff. Apply the same access and liveness checks to return and staff restoration. Specify what happens to in-flight orders when a sender dies, independently of prohibiting new orders from a dead issuer.

5. **High — Movement can crash after a smaller-than-threshold belief update.** `imperatores/systems/movement.py:112–151,182–185`. Approach keeps the old route if its destination changes by less than `approach_replan_m`. It can reach that stale endpoint outside the arrival tolerance for the updated point, pop the final waypoint, then index the empty route on the next tick. **Reproduce:** On flat ground start entity 1 at (500, 1500), `pace_jitter=0`. Give its belief an enemy foot contact at (550, 1500) using `belief.apply_sighting` and install an approach task to that handle with standoff 30. Step movement once, then update the same track to (570, 1500) and continue movement. The unit reaches x=520, its old destination. The route becomes `[]` and the next movement step raises `IndexError`. **Fix:** Treat exhausted routes explicitly. If the current belief implies a destination still outside tolerance, replan even below the ordinary movement threshold. Test a same-handle update near arrival with an offset between arrival tolerance and replan threshold.

6. **Medium — The order state machine accepts repeated delivery and records impossible movement as executed.** `imperatores/orders.py:81–86`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:172–196,242–253`, `imperatores/systems/movement.py:81–96`. With ordinary movement through the messenger loop I observed one delivery per rider. The lifecycle helper itself accepts any stage and can execute the same delivered message twice. Movement planning happens after the terminal success record. **Reproduce:** Send a hold between a commander and entity both at (500, 1500), step once, then call `command.deliver(w,w.messengers[0].message)` again. The order contains two delivered, understood and executed stages. Separately, issue a self advance to (-1, 1500) from (500, 1500). The input loads and the first tick records `order_executed` followed by `no_route`; the order remains executed. **Fix:** Enforce legal transitions and one terminal outcome, with idempotent delivery keyed by message ID. Validate place inputs and test route feasibility before marking execution successful, or distinguish task acceptance from execution and link task failure to the order.

7. **Medium — True identity can change a local handle even when the observer receives identical sightings.** `imperatores/belief.py:123–135,182–207`, `imperatores/systems/movement.py:64–69`. Stale links are removed only when their own true body reappears. The attribute matcher excludes still-linked handles, so it presents different identity evidence depending on the hidden ID. Existing approach orders act on that handle difference. **Reproduce:** In two copies of a one-officer world, use `belief.apply_sighting` to record unrecognised enemy foot ID 50 at (900, 1500) at tick 0. Advance the tick beyond `track_gap_s / tick_seconds`. In both worlds supply the same unrecognised sighting at (901, 1500), identical in every observable field. In A pass true ID 50, in B true ID 51. A reuses handle 1; B creates handle 2 and leaves stale handle 1. No true ID is printed in the view, but continuity exposes the distinction. Recognition can also delete a duplicate handle that an approach task still references. **Fix:** Age links out before associating any sightings, and resolve unrecognised associations by observable attributes and timing only. Publish a track merge redirect for active orders when recognition joins duplicates. Add a paired ID-swap test with identical observations after a gap.

8. **Medium — Replay omits command data that can change future behaviour and the audit record.** `imperatores/record.py:37–53,83–105`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:27–30`, `imperatores/systems/messengers.py:59–66`. The trace stores outbox IDs, input count, plan fired flags and order stage counts. It omits `w.seq`, messenger route goals and payloads, full plan and pending input content, and the private tracking links that affect order targeting and later association. The final digest also excludes the exported orders list. **Reproduce:** Execute an advance order. Modify the detail of its decided stage, such as `w.orders["1.1"].stages[0][2]["origin"]="changed"`. The exported order changes and `record.digest(w)` stays equal. Incrementing `w.seq[1]` leaves `record.state_bytes(w)` unchanged, though the next issued order and RNG key differ. **Fix:** Canonically hash the authoritative command state or its full mutations, including queued payloads, counters, typed transitions and tracking links. Include the exported order history in the final digest. Test equal scenario hashes and differing queue content, audit details and sequence counters.

9. **Medium — Signal response ignores mounted eligibility and has no stable default recall home.** `imperatores/orders.py:21–29`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:258–310`, `imperatores/scenario.py:192–195`. A plan keyed by signal name alone can be activated by any same-side emitter that reaches the recipient. A foot unit can comply with cavalry engage. A unit lacking a plan uses its live position for recall, effectively stopping where it is. **Reproduce:** Give foot entity 1 at (500, 1500) a plan binding `cavalry_engage` to advance to (700, 1500). Emit that signal from commander 900 at the default position. The foot unit starts the move on the first tick. Replace the plan with a moving unit without an explicit plan and emit recall mid-march. Its withdrawal target is its position at receipt. **Fix:** Enforce cavalry eligibility and define cue bindings by authorised emitter or deployment plan scope. Capture a stable deployment home for all entities, or explicitly define and record “halt here” for a unit without a home. Record an outcome for addressed recipients that cannot hear or comply, including destroyed units and unsupported recipient types.

10. **Medium — An unrelated same-issuer signal shifts a messenger's random draws.** `imperatores/systems/command.py:27–30,111,148,258–281`, `imperatores/systems/messengers.py:36–38,101–108`. Streams are separate, but signals, reports and orders share the issuer's sequence counter. **Reproduce:** Compare a single order from 900 to 1 on flat ground, seed 1, with the same fixture preceded at the same tick by an unaddressed advance signal from 900 with `to=[]`. The signal has no tactical effect with default misattribution. The same order's rider changes from key (900, 1) to (900, 2). At tick 0 the messenger uniform pairs are about (0.9385, 0.3045) and (0.8545, 0.5877). **Fix:** Use stable occurrence keys for exogenous inputs and derive child keys from parent plus recipient. Isolate sequence domains where appropriate. Preserve genuine resource contention for orders sharing staff while keeping RNG keys fixed for equivalent actions.

11. **Medium — Cyclic command structures load and can crash command resolution.** `imperatores/scenario.py:174–180`, `imperatores/systems/command.py:76–84,199–213,265–266`. The loader rules out direct self-reference but accepts indirect command cycles and duplicate subordinates. **Reproduce:** Give same-side commanders 900 and 910 command lists [910] and [900] and emit a recall to “all” from 900. The first engine tick raises `RecursionError`. A relayed input on this graph can repeatedly create children across ticks. **Fix:** Validate an acyclic command graph, valid rank edges and unique subordinates at load. Keep a visited guard in traversal and ensure a relay lineage cannot re-enter an ancestor.

## Information boundaries and test coverage

`describe_target` and `understand` choose using contact attributes. I found no branch that reads `intended` or a recorded `misread` to decide where a unit moves. The same mutable `Order` carries transport intent and true IDs for audit. Split decision-facing command data from audit metadata before exposing orders to step 5. `check_plans` reads the entity's own side and fit count, the clock and its local belief tracks. Its enemy test does not query enemy positions. The messenger system legitimately consults physical enemy presence for interception, subject to sight. Its physical observations should stay distinct from an officer's tactical information.

The tests exercise successful stage ordering, one queue that eventually drains, a ford detour, capture before delivery, a search timeout, misunderstood targets, a wait that succeeds or lapses, relay, several signal outcomes, and plans using sightings. They do not test indefinite travel, terminal-state uniqueness or replacement, authority, same-issuer paired input changes, or staff conservation under permanent losses. The moving-recipient test asserts execution but does not check where the messenger first rode. The report test checks provenance, but never changes the sender's picture while the report is in flight. The target-motion test checks one short interval. It does not compare full trajectories, per-tick task state or track reassociation with hidden identities swapped. The review 004 tests cover detached contact exports, report-induced belief hashes, current self-state, grazing and terrain immutability in normal use. The late reappearance tests do not cover the same-position identity swap in finding 7.

Step 4 parameters are labelled engineering assumptions with ranges. The messenger retargeting threshold of 20 m is a code literal. The fixture's subsequent movement preserved entity headcounts. I did not run CI, Python 3.10 or 3.13, cross-platform replay, exhaustive order state exploration, a full battle or historical calibration. I did not verify step 5 decision isolation, combat, formation footprints or the milestone gate. Returns under a moving sender deserve a dedicated regression when commander movement is introduced.
