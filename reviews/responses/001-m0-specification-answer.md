# Answer to review 001 — M0 specification

**By:** Claude, 22 November 2026. Each finding was checked against the draft and, where it cites a source, against the text. Changes are in `docs/M0_SPECIFICATION_DRAFT.md` (now v0.2).

**Source check:** Polybius 6.20–26 re-read in English (W. R. Paton's Loeb translation, LacusCurtius). Confirmed: 4,200 infantry and 300 cavalry per legion (20.8–9); 1,200 hastati, 1,200 principes, 600 triarii (21.9), velites being the remainder, with no figure stated; hastati carry two pila (23.8); ten maniples per class, two centurions and two optiones each, the first-elected commanding the right and either commanding the whole if the other is absent, velites divided equally among the maniples (24.1–7); ten turmae with three decurions each, command passing to the second if the first is absent (25.1–2); allied infantry usually equal to the Roman, allied cavalry about three times (26.7). Greek not collated.

| 1 | Unit count inconsistent with one legion plus allies | **Agreed.** My error: 30 maniples + 10 turmae is already 40 before velites and allies. | Tactical entities separated from administrative units. Default scenario is half a consular army (one legion, one allied contingent), about 80–100 entities per side. Cavalry and velites grouped into tactical entities where that is how they act. Section 4. |
| 2 | Contact model B is a strawman | **Agreed.** | Both models now allow local fluctuation within striking distance. The switch concerns only whether whole formations disengage substantially and re-engage without an explicit withdrawal. Both are calibrated to the same reference test, so the comparison isolates structure, not loss rates (also answers 12). Section 5.2. |
| 3 | Frequency gates could reward scripting or fail a good model | **Agreed, with one qualification.** Thresholds depend on the scenario. A random-event injector would also break the "nothing scripted" rule, so code review must catch it either way. | Frequencies moved to diagnostics. Acceptance now rests on controlled scenarios and interventions. A degenerate result (identical outcome in every run) is still flagged to Ben as a warning. Section 7. |
| 4 | Stronger counterfactual controls | **Agreed.** This is the best finding. | Paired-seed interventions added: change information only, delay or block one order, remove or move a reserve, change one officer's latitude, mirror forces and terrain. Added a technical requirement it implies: random draws keyed by system and entity, so an intervention does not reshuffle unrelated randomness. Information-integrity tests added (hidden unit; true state changes while belief does not). Section 7. |
| 5 | Recorded reasons versus proven causes | **Agreed.** | Each decision stores its input snapshot (beliefs, orders), the options considered with their score components, the selection, and links to the resulting orders and events. "Why?" is read from that record only. Where a causal claim needs more than the record (would X have happened without Y?), it is answered by a paired intervention run, not by narration. Section 6. |
| 6 | Order and resolve: keep distinct, avoid deterministic rules | **Agreed.** | Restricted retreat space now feeds several possible outcomes (crowding, inability to withdraw, desperate resistance, surrender, flight), not a single direction. Surrender added as an outcome. Section 5.1. |
| 7 | Polybius without a universal hierarchy | **Agreed.** Confirmed in 6.24–25. | Each entity has a tactical function and a command succession (for example two centurions per maniple, three decurions per turma), separate from the software's three command levels, which are labelled an abstraction. Carthaginian contingents get their own succession as Q001 informs. Section 5.1. |
| 8 | Minimal equipment model from M0 | **Agreed.** Confirmed: two pila per hastatus (6.23.8). | Small equipment model: reach class, missile type and stock (heavy pila limited and not replenished in pauses; light javelins and sling stones separate), protection class, mobility. Section 5.1. |
| 9 | Event-triggered decisions | **Agreed.** | Routine reassessment plus interrupts: unexpected contact, an order arriving, officer loss, a nearby rout, a threat to flank or rear. The interrupt prompts reconsideration; whether the officer notices and acts stays with the model. Section 5.6. |
| 10 | Explicit order failure modes | **Agreed.** | Failure at each stage listed: not sent, lost or delayed in transit, delivered but out of date, misunderstood because the recipient's picture differs, understood but not executable. Signals separated into emission, reception and compliance (Caesar BG 7.47–52 as a comparison, outside the period). Section 5.5. |
| 11 | Gate 9 assumes one direction for encirclement | **Agreed.** | Check removed; controlled scenario C5 tests that several consequences of restricted retreat can occur. |
| 12 | Sensitivity needs bounded ranges and controlled comparisons | **Agreed.** | Each parameter gets an explicit plausible range rather than ±50%; paired initial conditions; structural switches tested separately from parameter changes; results reported as model behaviour, never as historical validation. |
| 13 | Simplify the first viewer | **Agreed on sequencing.** The decision record is still built from the first step, because it cannot be added afterwards. | Viewer in two steps: playback plus an inspectable event log first; belief overlays and "Why?" navigation second. Section 6.1. |
| 14 | Termination is not victory | **Agreed.** | End state reported as battlefield conditions; a tactical verdict, which may be inconclusive, is given separately. Section 5.7. |

**On the recommended minimum gate:** adopted as the basis of the new section 7, with two additions. First, the mirror test (from finding 4) as an acceptance check. Second, a plausibility review: Ben (and ChatGPT if he wishes) watches a sample of replays and judges whether anything is absurd. Without it, a model could pass every integrity test and still produce battles no historian would recognise.

**Needs Ben's confirmation:** finding 1 raises the entity count from 25–40 to about 80–100 per side. That is consistent with his decision for maniple-scale units (the earlier count was simply wrong), but it is a change of size he should see.
